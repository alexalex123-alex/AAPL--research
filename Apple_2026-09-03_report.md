## Decision and user

This memo supports my decision about whether to buy Apple Inc. common stock for a long-term portfolio. This is my investment judgment, not a claim reported by Apple.

## Company and valuation date

Apple Inc. trades as AAPL on the Nasdaq Stock Market. [1]

I use October 31, 2025 as the valuation date because Apple filed its FY2025 Form 10-K on that date. [1]

## My initial thesis

My judgment is that Apple is an attractive long-term business because it has a large iPhone franchise, a faster-growing Services business, substantial liquidity, and an ongoing program of returning capital to shareholders. The factual evidence and source cross-checks supporting that judgment appear below.

## Evidence from the FY2025 filing

Apple generated FY2025 net sales of $416.161 billion, up 6% from FY2024. [1]

Apple generated FY2025 net income of $112.010 billion and diluted earnings per share of $7.46. [1]

iPhone was Apple's largest product category, producing $209.586 billion of FY2025 net sales, a 4% year-over-year increase. [1]

Services produced $109.158 billion of FY2025 net sales, growing 14% year over year; Apple attributed the increase primarily to advertising, the App Store, and cloud services. [1]

Apple held $35.934 billion in cash and cash equivalents and $96.486 billion in marketable securities at September 27, 2025. [1]

Apple carried $90.678 billion of term debt and $7.979 billion of commercial paper at fiscal year-end. [1]

Apple repurchased $90.052 billion of common stock and declared $15.413 billion in dividends and dividend equivalents during FY2025. [1]

Greater China net sales were $64.377 billion, down 4% from FY2024, while total company sales increased. [1]

## Prior-filing and recent-news cross-check

In both FY2023 and FY2024, management stated that Apple was focused on expanding its market opportunities in smartphones, personal computers, tablets, wearables and accessories, and services. [2] [3]

Services net sales increased from $85.200 billion in FY2023 to $96.169 billion in FY2024 and $109.158 billion in FY2025. [1] [2] [3]

Total net sales increased from $383.285 billion in FY2023 to $391.035 billion in FY2024 and $416.161 billion in FY2025. [1] [2] [3]

This evidence supports my judgment that Apple made progress in expanding its Services opportunity, but it does not prove that every management objective was achieved. This is my interpretation of the reported results. [1] [2] [3]

Apple's October 30, 2025 press release reported September-quarter revenue of $102.5 billion, up 8% year over year, and described record September-quarter revenue for total company sales and iPhone and an all-time revenue record for Services. [4]

## Post-valuation leadership update

Apple announced on April 20, 2026 that John Ternus would become CEO effective September 1, 2026 and that Tim Cook would become executive chair. Apple said the Board unanimously approved the transition after a long-term succession-planning process. [5]

Ternus joined Apple’s product design team in 2001, became vice president of Hardware Engineering in 2013, and joined the executive team in 2021. Apple states that he has overseen hardware engineering across its product categories, including iPad, AirPods, iPhone, Mac, and Apple Watch; before Apple, he worked as a mechanical engineer at Virtual Research Systems and earned a mechanical-engineering degree from the University of Pennsylvania. [5]

My interpretation is that Ternus’s hardware-engineering background could increase emphasis on product execution, reliability, durability, and hardware innovation. Tim Cook’s continued role as executive chair could reduce transition risk, but this is an inference, not a company forecast. [5]

This leadership change happened after the October 31, 2025 valuation date, so I treat it as a monitoring item rather than using it to justify the original valuation decision. I will watch for changes in product strategy, Services priorities, capital allocation, China performance, and regulatory engagement under Ternus.
## My consequential assumptions

I assume iPhone remains Apple's largest revenue contributor and that Services continues to grow faster than hardware. This is my forward-looking assumption; it is informed by FY2025 iPhone sales of $209.586 billion and Services growth of 14%. [1]

I assume Apple can sustain enough free-cash-flow generation to continue repurchases and dividends. This is my forward-looking assumption; FY2025 repurchases and dividends provide historical evidence of Apple's capital-return policy. [1]

I assume the Greater China sales decline does not become a sustained drag on overall growth. This is my judgment about future performance; FY2025 Greater China sales declined 4%. [1]

## Important unknowns and research plan

I do not yet know whether AAPL's market price on October 31, 2025 provided a sufficient margin of safety relative to intrinsic value. This is unknown because the cited filings and press release do not provide a market-price comparison or an intrinsic-value calculation.

I will record AAPL's closing share price, market capitalization, and enterprise value on the valuation date; forecast revenue, margins, taxes, reinvestment, and free cash flow; calculate cost of equity and WACC; build base, upside, and downside DCF cases; and cross-check the result with P/E, EV/EBIT, and EV/FCF multiples.

## Final call

**Buy.** This is my judgment based on Apple's scale, profitability, Services growth, liquidity, and capital returns, as evidenced above. [1] [2] [3] [4]

I would change this call to watch or do not initiate if a valuation shows AAPL's market price exceeds a reasonable estimate of intrinsic value, or if sustained Services deceleration and deeper China weakness materially reduce expected free-cash-flow growth.
